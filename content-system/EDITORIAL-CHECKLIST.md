# Editorial checklist

Use this with the technical-review skill before publishing an article, demo guide, benchmark or audit summary.

1. **Start with the reader's task.** Say what they can do or decide. Explain unfamiliar terms when first needed.
2. **Name the action.** Prefer “run the query” to “enable a seamless evaluation experience.” Identify who acts: the application, model, service or developer.
3. **Check every material claim.** Record its source, version and date. Label observations, interpretations and proposed tests separately.
4. **Keep the limits attached to the result.** Include workload size and relevant conditions. Token savings do not establish total-cost savings. An import or mock test does not establish service behavior.
5. **Remove filler.** Cut generic praise, repeated conclusions, unsupported superlatives and unnecessary introductions. Keep technical precision even when it needs an extra sentence.
6. **Use tables for comparison.** Use paragraphs to explain a decision. Avoid packing a complete article into wide table cells.
7. **Recheck after editing.** Compare figures, finding IDs, status, versions and links with the evidence. Keep Markdown and any structured exports consistent.
8. **Record the review.** State what was checked, what changed and what remains untested. A human reviewer must give their own approval; do not infer it from automated checks.

The standard is clear, accurate writing. Do not claim that style proves who wrote a passage, or that a detector score establishes quality.
